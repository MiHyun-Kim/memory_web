GREEN
FIELD
The greenfield behind my neighbors house was where i spent all my time. Expansive and evergreen, filled with tall grasses, a few copses of trees, and a creek with cattails that grew along its bed. Time there was precious and fleeting, though then it didn't feel that way. The height of my memories were during 2008. We would stamp down the cattails, making a hidden forest between their towering stems. A secret world hovering above the creek we thought would never stop flowing. But then drought came, time progressed, and we moved on. The greenfield became a memory, a cut-through on walks when we needed to save time, or the hangout spot for those teenagers who were up to no good. Mason jars and tinfoil would be found - crude bongs left behind for the new 5th graders to find and ask questions about. Eventually the cattails died, the poison ivy grew, and we became those teenagers. We took back the field, found new ways to be there, but time doesn't stop, and eventually we grew out of it again. Covid came one day, and suddenly we sat there again, with our parents dogs, wondering what had happened. When did we become struggling adults, standing in a field, with dogs that weren't ours? We had graduated, found jobs, and yet here we were again, back home, back in the field, back to being kids again.
