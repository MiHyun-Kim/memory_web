THE OBJECT
THE OBJECT I ASSOCIATE MOST WITH MY CHILDHOOD IS MY DOLLHOUSE. IT WAS A SMALL, PORTABLE PLASTIC COTTAGE—LESS THAN A FOOT TALL—WITH COLORFUL IMAGERY, TINY FURNITURE, AND ROOMS THAT FOLDED OPEN AND CLOSED.
BEFORE IT WAS MINE
LONG BEFORE I PLAYED WITH IT, IT HAD BELONGED TO MY OLDEST SISTER, WHO USED IT UNTIL SHE OUTGREW IT. THEN IT WAS PASSED DOWN TO MY OTHER SISTER.
MARKS
OF USE
BY THE TIME IT REACHED ME, THE DOLLHOUSE ALREADY CARRIED SIGNS OF ITS HISTORY—STICKERS ON THE WALLS AND A FEW SCRATCHES LEFT FROM YEARS OF PLAY.
SHARED CHILDHOODS
WHEN IT FINALLY BECAME MINE, THE DOLLHOUSE HELD THE QUIET HISTORY OF MORE THAN ONE CHILDHOOD.
BEDROOM CORNER
THE DOLLHOUSE LIVED IN THE CORNER OF MY BEDROOM, ALWAYS EASY TO REACH. IT BECAME A SPACE OF COMFORT AND DISTRACTION.
PORTABLE WORLD
BECAUSE IT WAS PORTABLE, I OFTEN CARRIED IT FROM ROOM TO ROOM, OR BROUGHT IT WITH ME TO RELATIVES’ HOMES, SETTING IT UP WHEREVER I COULD FIND SPACE.
MISSING PIECES
OVER TIME, MANY OF THE ORIGINAL FURNITURE PIECES AND DOLLS DISAPPEARED OR WERE FORGOTTEN.
IMAGINED STORIES
EVEN WITHOUT EVERYTHING IT ONCE CAME WITH, THE DOLLHOUSE NEVER FELT INCOMPLETE. I SPENT HOURS OPENING ITS WALLS, ARRANGING WHAT REMAINED, AND INVENTING STORIES FOR THE DOLLS WHO LIVED INSIDE.
WHAT IT BECAME
THAT LITTLE HAND-ME-DOWN HOUSE WAS NOT JUST A TOY. IT BECAME AN EARLY LESSON IN IMAGINATION AND CREATIVE POSSIBILITY.
